I use Apollo.io for prospecting on my own pipeline, on the free plan, and I
went in expecting to drive it through the API. That is not how it works, and
the boundary is not obvious from the pricing page.

This is what I found from a working account. Everything below reflects the
plan structure as of August 2026, and pricing pages change, so verify the
numbers against theirs before making a decision on them.

## The short version

You can search, filter and view prospect data in the web interface on the free
plan. You cannot drive prospecting search programmatically. The search API and
the enrichment context features sit behind a paid tier, and there is no
partial or metered access to them from free.

That single distinction is the one that matters, because it decides whether
your prospecting workflow can be automated at all or has to stay manual.

## Credits are two different things, and the difference bites

This is the part that confused me longest, so it is worth stating plainly.

**Lead credits** are what you spend to reveal a contact's details inside the
interface. On the free plan you get an allowance of these, and they work as
advertised. You run a search, you find someone who fits, you reveal them, you
see the contact information.

**Export credits** are what you spend to get that data *out* in bulk, as a
file. These are a separate pool with a separate allowance, and on my free
account that pool is zero.

So the free plan lets you find and see prospects, and does not let you bulk
extract them. You can read the data. You cannot easily take it with you at
volume. Those feel like the same capability until you try to build a pipeline
on top of the second one.

## What the paid step up actually costs

Getting programmatic search access is not a small increment. At the time of
writing it means the Organization plan, which is around $119 per user per
month and carries a three seat minimum.

That is the number people miss. It is not "$119 to unlock the API." The seat
minimum makes the real annual figure meaningfully larger, and for a solo
operator or a two person team the extra seats are pure overhead bought to
reach a feature.

## What I decided, and why

I stayed on free.

The reasoning is not that the paid tier is bad value. For a team running
volume outbound with several people in the tool daily, it is probably
straightforwardly worth it. The reasoning is that I am pre-revenue, and a
recurring cost bought to speed up an unvalidated process is exactly the kind
of spend that feels like progress while being none.

The honest trade is that my sourcing is slower and manual. I do not think it
is much worse in quality, and it may be slightly better, because the manual
pass means I actually look at every company rather than scoring a list I never
read. That is not an argument for staying manual forever. It is an argument
for staying manual until there is revenue to weigh the cost against.

## The manual workflow that replaces the API

If you land in the same position, this is the loop that works:

1. **Search in the web interface**, using the firmographic filters, which are
   genuinely good and are not the gated part.
2. **Filter on a real trigger rather than static attributes.** In my case that
   is job postings: a company hiring for a role whose duties are the thing I
   would automate. This is the single highest-signal filter I have found, and
   it works the same on free as on paid.
3. **Reveal the contacts you actually want** with lead credits, rather than
   revealing broadly and filtering after. Credits are the scarce resource, so
   the qualification decision has to happen before the reveal, not after.
4. **Copy the list out manually** into whatever you score with. This is the
   step the export credits would have automated, and it is genuinely tedious.
5. **Score and draft outside Apollo**, against a written profile of who you
   sell to, then send from your own mail account.

Step four is the tax. Everything else is roughly what I would do anyway.

## What I would tell someone deciding

Pay for it when the constraint is genuinely throughput. If you have a
repeatable process that converts, and the only limit is how many good-fit
companies you can get in front of per week, then the seat cost is trivially
worth it and you should stop reading.

Do not pay for it to accelerate a process you have not validated. Automating
outbound before you know which segment replies means you will simply reach a
wrong conclusion faster and with a subscription attached.

The intermediate position, which is where I am: run manual until a segment
demonstrably works, then automate the segment that works rather than the whole
funnel. The manual phase also produces something the API phase does not, which
is that you have personally read every company you contacted, and that shows
up in the copy.

I use the manual list as the input to a scoring system I built for my own
pipeline. That part, and specifically where it fails, is written up in
[scoring B2B leads with an LLM](/blog/scoring-b2b-leads-with-an-llm).
